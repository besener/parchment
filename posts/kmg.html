A Teaching Manifesto

August 23, 2025

I read a couple of books on pedagogy this summer. One of them was “Radical Hope: A Teaching Manifesto,” a book by Kevin M. Gannon, published in 2020 by West Virginia University Press, is part of the series Teaching and Learning in Higher Education, edited by James Lang.

Gannon paints a bold and inspiring picture of what higher education could become. He champions an inclusive teaching philosophy that tackles the real-world challenges students and faculty face, from financial strain and mental health struggles to barriers in accessibility and persistent inequalities. He shines a spotlight on Universal Design for Learning (UDL), urging educators to reinvent their courses with a rich variety of materials and assessments, and to nurture 'brave spaces' where honest, transformative conversations can flourish. In Gannon’s view, higher education is a vibrant landscape for sowing the seeds of 'radical hope,' where teaching and learning spark agency, equity, and a deeper sense of our shared humanity. For him, radical hope means daring to reach for a brighter tomorrow, even when its outline is still coming into focus. Teaching, in Gannon’s eyes, is itself a radical act of hope, a living testament to the belief in a better world and a guide for students to break through boundaries.

Gannon’s book resonated with me on a deep level, its clarity and sincerity echoing my own hopes and ideals. Discovering my beliefs reflected in his words filled me with a renewed sense of “radical hope”. Like many educators in higher education, I am continually seeking innovative ways to uplift and empower my students. We strive to create lifelong learning journeys, inviting students to become true partners in the pursuit of knowledge. By championing inclusivity, we open doors to greater accessibility, comfort, and a sense of belonging, transforming theory into meaningful, lived experience.

I wholeheartedly recommend Gannon’s book to every teacher and graduate student eager to delve further into pedagogy and contemporary higher education.
